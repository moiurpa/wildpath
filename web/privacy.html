Privacy policy

Effective 16 November 2026 · Draft, pending legal review

WildPath is a free app that lets drivers report animals killed on the road and warns other drivers about risky stretches. This page explains, in plain English, what we collect and what we do with it.

The short version

  • We don't ask for your name, email, phone number or an account.
  • When you report an animal, we save what animal, where and when.
  • We do not record where you drive. Your location stays on your phone, except the spot of each animal you report.
  • Reports are shared publicly on the map without anything that identifies you.
  • We may share or license report data, without anything that identifies you, to help protect animals and drivers.

What we collect when you report

  • The kind of animal (for example deer, raccoon, "not sure").
  • The location of the animal, and how accurate your phone's GPS was.
  • The date and time, and whether it was dawn, day, dusk or night there.
  • Your speed and direction at that moment, if your phone provides them. This helps place the animal on the right side of the road.
  • How the report was made (website, phone, voice, camera) and the app version.
  • Your answers to "Still there?" questions about other reports.
  • A random code created by the app on your phone. We never store the code itself, only a scrambled ("hashed") version, so we can count duplicates and stop spam. It is not linked to your name, phone number or device.

What we don't collect

  • No account, name, email, contacts or photos (unless a future version lets you attach a photo, and you choose to).
  • No record of your trips. Drive mode uses your location on your phone to warn you; it is not sent to us.
  • No advertising trackers, and we don't sell ads.

Services we use

  • Google Maps shows the map. Google receives the usual information any website sends when it loads a map, such as your IP address. See Google's privacy policy.
  • Supabase stores the reports (servers in the United States).
  • GitHub Pages hosts the website.
  • OpenStreetMap / Nominatim answers address searches you type in the search box.
  • Open-Meteo provides the weather for animal warnings (frogs on rainy nights, nesting turtles, deer in deep snow and others). While drive mode is on, it receives your location rounded to about 1 km, about every 30 minutes.

How report data is used and shared

  • To show reports and hotspots on the public map and warn drivers.
  • To study where, when and which animals are hit, and to improve the warnings.
  • To share or license report data with organizations that work on road and wildlife safety, such as state transportation and wildlife agencies, researchers, insurers, navigation and car companies. What we share never includes the scrambled device code.

Location data can sometimes reveal patterns about a person even without a name. That is why we never publish who made a report, and why shared data leaves out the device code.

How long we keep it

Reports are kept as long as they are useful for road and wildlife safety research, which can be many years. The map shows only recent reports.

Your choices

  • You can use the map without reporting anything.
  • You can turn off location in your phone's settings; you'll then place reports by tapping the map.
  • You can clear the app's data in your browser settings, which deletes the random code from your phone.
  • To ask about or remove reports you made, contact us (below). Because we don't know who you are, we may need the approximate time and place of the report to find it.

Children

WildPath is meant for drivers and passengers and is not directed at children under 13.

Changes

If we change this policy, we'll update this page and its date.

Contact
