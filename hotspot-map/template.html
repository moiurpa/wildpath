    How the risk is estimated

    Risk = where × when. “Where” is the state's Wildlife Crossing Value for each road segment: , higher means wildlife crosses there more. “When” multiplies that by a seasonal factor and a time-of-day factor for the animal you pick.

    The seasonal and time factors are estimates from published patterns, not Vermont crash counts: deer peak during the November rut (Vermont Fish & Wildlife) and at dawn and dusk; Maine DOT reports moose collisions peak in May and June. Small animals such as raccoons, skunks and opossums are mostly active at night. They will be replaced with Vermont data.

    What this map does not show yet

    • Actual crashes or roadkill reports. This is a prediction from habitat, not a record of hits.
    • Traffic volume. A busy road with the same score is riskier; VTrans traffic counts come next.
    • Town roads. The state data covers Interstates, US and VT routes only ( road miles).

    Source: Vermont Open Geodata Portal, VT Wildlife Crossing Value, Vermont Agency of Natural Resources.
